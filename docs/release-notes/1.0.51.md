## The keys a phone's keyboard lacks

Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt, Shift, the arrows, Home, End, Page up and Page down,
sent to the field being typed in: a terminal, an SSH app, or a web editor
such as vscode.dev or code-server in the browser. Two Android apps, built
from the same code, which can be on one phone together, and Berpiztu AI
Keyboard for Windows:

- **KeyPlus** (`KeyPlus-Popup.apk`): the keys in a strip right above
  whatever keyboard is open. It works beside the keyboard you already use;
  it does not replace it.
- **Berpiztu AI Keyboard** (`Berpiztu-AI-Keyboard.apk`): a keyboard of its
  own, Gboard's copied, for any Android phone, tablet or headset (Galaxy
  XR).
- **Berpiztu AI Keyboard for Windows** (`Berpiztu-AI-Keyboard-Windows.msi`):
  the same keyboard in a card that floats over every window, or docked at
  the bottom of the screen.

## Berpiztu AI Keyboard, as Gboard

- **The keyboards it comes with**, in Gboard's look: **Standard**, Gboard's
  keys, and **Standard PC**, a PC's: F1–F12, Insert, an ISO Enter, Home,
  End, Page up and Page down around the arrows, ´ and ` as dead keys, Caps
  Lock, Ctrl, Alt and ⊞ on until the next key, a PC's keys repeating while
  held. Each key of Standard PC shows, as a PC's keycap, what it types with
  Shift in its top corner and with Alt Gr in its bottom one; with Shift or
  Alt Gr on, the keys show only what they type then.
- **Shortcuts as a physical keyboard's**: with Ctrl, Alt or ⊞ on, a
  character goes out as the key a keyboard of the language has for it, on
  Android and Windows alike: Ctrl+ç is the Spanish keyboard's, VS Code's
  comment, as Ctrl+/ is in English.
- **Sound on keypress**, in Preferences beside Haptic feedback, plays
  Android's keypress sounds, the space's, the delete's and Enter's their
  own; off unless turned on, as Gboard's.
- **On the Galaxy XR, Standard XR**: Gboard's keyboard there, a tablet's
  keys with Tab, Caps Lock, ⇧ at both ends and ◀ ▶ by the space bar, its
  keys round and apart, a card filling the headset's box with a band to
  hold it, and the number pad of Gboard's on the headset.
- **1234**, the number pad, from the symbols' page, as Gboard's.
- **Back to the letters**: on ?123 or =\<, a space or Enter after a symbol
  goes back to the letters, as Gboard's, on Android and Windows alike.
- **The case of the text selected**: on Android, Shift tapped twice turns
  it all into capitals, then small letters, then its first letter a
  capital, and round again, the text still selected.
- **Keyboards by language**, Spanish and English: each screen shows one
  keyboard in each, docked and floating alike, and 🌐 changes the language
  keeping the kind in sight, Standard or Standard PC.
- **Settings › Theme**: the language and the keyboard as two lists, a name
  with **Save as** and **Delete**, and the keyboard drawn as it shows,
  designed right there.
- **Colours**: **System**, **Light**, **Dark**, or **Custom**, the
  keyboard's colour and the accent's chosen, one step back with ↶; with
  **Per app**, each application its own colours or its window's (**App's**),
  the keyboard changing as you go from one to another. On Android they
  stand in three lines, a long application's name cut short in its list
  and shown whole when it opens.
- **⊞ › Design** designs a standard keyboard, on the keyboard or in Theme: a
  key marked is made narrower or wider, dragged elsewhere, or taken out; **+
  Key** adds Esc, Tab, Delete, Insert, Home, End, Page up, Page down, Ctrl,
  Alt, ⊞, Alt Gr, F1–F12 or an arrow; **+ Row** adds a row and **+ Column**
  a column; a marked Enter tapped again takes the next of its shapes (one
  row, ISO's L, the old L, square). Done saves it as a keyboard of its own,
  docked and floating each with its own design where it was designed so.
- **Resize** sizes and moves the keyboard; **Reset** brings it
  back as made, **Cancel** as it was. A size is kept for each: Standard and
  Standard PC, floating and docked, the screen landscape and portrait.
- **A letter held** opens Gboard's popup: the symbol in its corner and the
  letter's accents (á à ä â …, ñ, ç), the finger sliding to one and lifting
  to type it, on Android and Windows alike.
- **Gboard's toolbar and grid** (⊞), customised with **⊞ › ✏**: a button
  held is dragged between the toolbar and the grid. They offer only what
  the keyboard does, **Spelling** and **Glide typing** among them, lit
  while on; GIF, stickers, split, translate and the like are gone.
- **Troubleshooting › the keyboard's log**, as on Windows: every warning
  and error with its time, **Detailed log** for what the keyboard follows,
  what the log keeps word for word with **Refresh** and **Clear the log**,
  and **Save a copy of the log** to send it.
- **🎤 while it listens** shows the voice coming in, as Gboard's: bars in
  the microphone's place rising and falling as you speak, on Android and
  Windows alike.
- **Voice typing › Microphone**, as on Windows, for ia-voice: the system's
  or a headset's, USB or Bluetooth one; the silence and the volume say
  what they do.
- **Swipe typing**: a word swiped over the letters is typed, read by FUTO
  Swipe; its other likely words wait in the toolbar, and a tap takes one in
  its place. ⌫ right after it deletes the whole word, as Gboard's, so a word
  read wrong goes at once; **Glide typing › Delete a swiped word whole**
  turns that off, on Android and Windows alike.
- **Settings › Dictionary**, your own words, kept on the device and sent
  nowhere: a word no word list has (a web domain, a name) is swiped and
  offered as you type, first; added by hand, or learnt with a tap on it at
  the toolbar's left, where a word no list has shows as typed (**Offer new
  words to learn**; nothing is learnt by itself), each with ✏ to
  write it again and a red ✕ to delete it. **Export the dictionary** and
  **Import a dictionary** take your words from one device to another, and
  import Gboard's too; imported words are added, none taken away.
- **Spelling by language**, on Android and Windows: **Languages**,
  **Spelling**, **Dictionary** and **Glide typing** list the languages, and
  **More options** opens a language's page, with its Spelling, its Glide
  typing and its words. **Correct words**, Spanish and
  English, off until turned on, when its LibreOffice dictionary is
  downloaded once, corrects a misspelled word as it ends ("kasa": "casa",
  "helo": "hello"), writes with its capital a word only written so ("i":
  "I", "english": "English", "españa": "España"), and offers what it may
  have meant as you type, your
  dictionary's words among them; **Autocorrect** writes the corrections at
  once, ↶ taking them back, or offers them with ✓. Spanish also has **Use
  the Wikipedia's common misspellings** (21,000, after the keyboard's own:
  "hai": hay, ahí, ay; "as" offers "has", "haz" without changing),
  **Correct accents with the model** (diactag, 38 MB: "cómo estás") and
  **Put ¿ and ¡ at the start**. **Settings › Spelling** turns all of it on
  and off at once, as the toolbar's spellcheck button does (off to write
  code), and **Download again** fetches what it downloaded again, for a file
  damaged. What a language lacks, the toolbar says, with **Download**
  beside it. **Capital at the start of a sentence**, off until turned on and
  needing no download, puts Shift on by itself after . ? or ! and a space
  and on a new line, on Android and Windows alike. Nothing is sent
  anywhere.
- **Nothing is carried that is not used**, on Android and Windows:
  **Glide typing**, off until turned on for a language, then downloads
  FUTO Swipe's model and that language's word list (4.7 MB) from their
  sources; the word lists also give the words offered as you type. Neither
  the APK nor the installer carries a dictionary, a word list or a model,
  and nothing is downloaded until a choice that needs it is turned on.
- **The box to try the keys in**, over the settings' pages, on Windows as on
  Android.
- **🎤 falls back to local**: when the local network's or the remote speech
  service does not answer (no network, the server down), the keyboard
  listens locally and says so.
- **Learn from the words I choose**: a word taken from the toolbar in place
  of a swiped one is typed first the next time that swipe reads the same,
  so the keyboard follows how you swipe ("está" over "esta");
  **Forget the words chosen** undoes it.
- **Words as you type**: while a word is typed letter by letter, the
  toolbar offers the commonest words that start so, accents aside
  ("cafe" offers "café"); a tap writes it with a space after it.
- **🎤 voice typing**: the words appear in the field as they are heard,
  until 🎤 is tapped again or, if **Voice typing** says so, after a
  silence. Local is Android's own recognizer; **Local network** is ia-voice
  (NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 ASR) on a computer at home, by its IP; **Remote** is
  a published one, with the user and password its proxy asks for, kept
  sealed on the device.
- **Emoji**: the comma held, or ⊞'s Emojis, opens them in the keys' place,
  by group, those typed lately first, and the latest in a row over the
  toolbar.
- **Moving out of the way**: a floating keyboard over the field being typed
  in moves above or below it; **Also move out of the way of menus** does the
  same for an open menu, through an accessibility service of the
  keyboard's own, which also sees an application's colour for **App's**.
- **What was copied** is kept to paste again, images and screenshots too;
  each one's menu, Paste, Pin or Unpin, Edit and Delete, has Material
  Symbols' signs in the text's colour, as Gboard's.
- **On the headset** the keyboard is the phone's, as any Android
  keyboard; the headset's own, filling the box the headset gives a
  keyboard, is an option in Preferences.
- **Both apps turn themselves on** over the device's own Wireless
  debugging, no computer needed.

## Berpiztu AI Keyboard for Windows

- The same keyboards, Standard and Standard PC, in Spanish and English, in
  a card over every window that never takes the focus from the one typed
  in: its keys reach that window as Windows' own.
- **Design**, as on Android, and the keyboards designed kept by language;
  **Theme** with the keyboard drawn as it shows, designed there one to one.
- **Dock** docks the keyboard at the bottom of the screen, the other
  windows making room for it; **Resize** sizes and moves it, docked
  or floating, a size kept for each: Standard and Standard PC, floating
  and docked, the screen landscape and portrait.
- **Colours**, as on Android, **Per app** taking each window's colour as it
  shows.
- Swipe typing, words as you type, emoji in colour, and 🎤 with ia-voice
  (this computer's, one on the local network or a remote one), the words
  typed as they are heard.
- It shows when a text field takes the focus and hides, becomes its ⌨ icon
  or stays when the focus leaves; it moves out of the way of the text and of
  open menus, VS Code's included once it is started as **Troubleshooting**
  explains; ✕ hides it, and **Quit**, by Windows' clock, ends it.
- After ✕, tapping or clicking the text again brings the keyboard back, as
  on Android; typing on the computer's own keyboard leaves it hidden.
- Coming back to VS Code's editor from another window shows the keyboard
  again: VS Code tells only a pane focused, and the keyboard, seeing the
  editor's caret still shown, stayed hidden.
- Its settings are an app of their own, as on Android: Languages,
  Preferences, Theme, Glide typing, Voice typing,
  Troubleshooting and About.
- Its keys always reach the window typed in. Come to from the taskbar, a
  window could lose the focus to the keyboard on the first click on a key,
  and the keys then typed nothing; the keyboard now never takes it.
- A key held (⌫, the arrows, Standard PC's keys, the emoji's ⌫) stops as
  the keyboard hides or lets go of the mouse or the finger, instead of
  going on typing out of sight.
- A window whose accessibility answers with an error no longer ends the
  keyboard: the error is written in its log, and any that would end it is
  written there first.
- The installer closes the keyboard and its settings when they are
  running, so it installs over them: asked to close, the keyboard only hid,
  and the installer could not replace it.
- The keyboard no longer stands still while another application is slow
  to answer: what the keyboard asks it, to know where the text and the
  menus are, is asked apart from its keys, so a VS Code window connecting
  by SSH held it for half a minute and no longer does.

## New versions, from About

- **Check for updates automatically**, on unless turned off, asks
  GitHub once a day whether a new version is out, and **Check now** asks
  at once. A new one asks whether to update; nothing is installed without
  a yes.
- On Windows the question is a window, and a yes downloads the installer
  and opens it, which closes the keyboard and starts the new one.
- On Android it is a notification that opens the app, which asks; a yes
  downloads the app's own APK and hands it to Android's installer, which
  asks too, and the first time asks to allow installing from the app. The
  popup has **Check now** on its screen.

## Where the keyboard stands, from its app

- **Back**, the gesture or ←, goes to the page you came from, and from that
  to the one before; the list of settings stays where it was scrolled, and
  each setting's name stands out in bold.

- **Pop-up keyboard** puts the keyboard in a window of its own, or docks it
  again, on every screen; an open keyboard takes it at once.
- **Reset the keyboard**, after asking, brings it back as first installed.
- The keyboard's app has a box to try the keys in, clear of Android 15's
  bars, and opens on its two first steps while the keyboard is not on or
  not in use.

## The popup, on a headset and on INAIR's pod

- The popup no longer hangs on INAIR's pod: Android takes five seconds there
  to say where every window is, and that is now asked away from the app's
  main work.
- Its corner grips are large, with diagonal arrows drawn, for a headset's
  pointer reaches them as a finger does.
- KeyPlus turns its own service on where it may write the secure settings.

## Installing

On Android, download `KeyPlus-Popup.apk`, `Berpiztu-AI-Keyboard.apk` or both
from this page on the phone or the headset, and open them there: each
carries everything it runs, and installs over the version before. On
Windows, run `Berpiztu-AI-Keyboard-Windows.msi`: it installs for you alone,
no administrator asked, closes the keyboard and its settings if they are
running, starts the keyboard, and starts it out of sight at every sign-in.
The README says how to turn each on.
